# Publication integrity and independent assessment, 2026-10-02

This record separates implemented engineering controls, completed assessment evidence, and external configuration. The remediation changes are under review. The independent machine assessment and local validation are complete. The reconstructed sample supports a narrow excerpt-grounding result; repair regressions and selection omissions prevent a broader quality claim. No deployment-reliability estimate is claimed.

## Implemented controls

[Publication resolution](../../agent_runner/publication.py) shares the accepted-repair policy with publication preparation and the weekly monitor. Candidate, evidence, references, corpus, configuration, and Markdown hashes must agree before a topic enters review. Frozen evidence positions must align with the selected candidate, whose exact rendered briefing and validation footer must match the published Markdown. Monitoring requires a receipt identifying the artifact actually deployed and independent confirmation of the matching Actions attempt's successful Pages deployment. Merely retaining a `ready` diagnostic run does not establish publication.

[Weekly sampling](../../evaluator/production_grounding.py) uses an explicit ISO report-date week and keeps the latest verified successful deployment per report date. It records duplicate, superseded, unavailable, out-of-window, and unverifiable candidates instead of treating the latest seven artifacts as a complete week. [Historical monitor rates](grounding-monitor.md) remain unchanged and carry their earlier publication-identity and sampling limitations.

[Judgment checkpoints](../../evaluator/judge_io.py) bind effective prompt bytes, reviewer provider/model, exposed generation controls, and timeout. [Semantic review](../../evaluator/semantic_review.py) and [quality review](../../evaluator/quality.py) also bind referenced frozen inputs; semantic topics must match the parsed final output. Generation-owned trial-corpus and final-output digests must verify before a fresh or resumed judgment; older generation rows without those digests are explicitly unverifiable and preserved. Existing labels do not become another reviewer's work. A compatible directory resumes the same assessment; a new empty directory creates an independent assessment. Legacy checkpoints are preserved and rejected, deterministic labels retain their identity, and aggregates retain the actual reviewer for each judgment.

[Provider transport](../../agent_runner/http_transport.py) bounds success and error response bodies and enforces an elapsed deadline across connection, reading, and retries. Operational metadata validation distinguishes missing or invalid billing from observed zero, rejects negative or nonfinite cost and invalid token counts, and retains usable telemetry on typed failures. Redirect restrictions and the policy against retrying ambiguously completed paid calls remain part of the transport boundary.

[Daily publication](../../daily_publish.py) reports generation or preparation failure through a nonzero outcome while allowing the workflow to deploy public failure records. A successful site deployment and a successful briefing generation are separate operational outcomes.

## Independent quality assessment

The [frozen protocol](../../evaluator/protocols/independent-production-2026-10-02.json) specified reproducible topic sampling, blinded reviewers separate from generation and repair, double review, material-claim support, citation relevance, clarity, completeness, usefulness, repair comparisons, and corpus-level omissions. Assessment covers supplied feed excerpts, not full articles. Machine review is not human validation, and reviewers using the same model may share blind spots.

The original target was roughly 30–40 topics across at least five recent, verifiably published dates, plus available repair pairs. Private historical diagnostics could not be decrypted because the archive passphrase was unavailable. The protocol was amended before assessment to add reconstructed daily feed corpora for testing. Those corpora are not the evidence used for historical publication and cannot support a historical deployment-reliability claim.

The reconstructed window is September 25 through October 1, 2026 UTC, ending at October 2 00:00 UTC. Retained item counts by date are 5, 1, 1, 6, 6, 49, and 194. These unequal daily corpora make available-topic and selection denominators necessary; missing material cannot be filled with invented topics.

The single retrospective fetch retained 262 items with no reported fetch errors. Source caps and recency skew produced sparse older dates. Each partition explicitly inherits weekly source health; these are not seven independently fetched daily snapshots. Current production configuration and prompts produced 57 topics through selection, frozen evidence, prose, and deterministic validation. Generation used a `gpt-6.1-sol` high-reasoning subagent instead of the production OpenRouter fallback chain, without a Jev pass or additional paid API calls. Temperature and seed are unavailable, not assumed.

The SHA-256 ordering in the protocol selected up to six topics per date: **29 topics across seven dates**, short of the target of 36. Daily generated/sample counts were 4/4, 1/1, 1/1, 6/6, 5/5, 18/6, and 22/6. No reconstructed date was excluded; 28 generated topics were outside the topic sample. Sparse sections remained underfilled. The **verified historical publication denominator is zero**.

Fresh `gpt-6.1-sol` high-reasoning reviewers assessed the frozen packets independently of generation and repair. Packets hid generator identity, earlier scores, and repair correspondence. The primary reviewer judged all 29 sampled topics supported, with no unsafe grouping or irrelevant citations. The second reviewer assessed 10 of those 29 topics and agreed on support, grouping, and citation relevance for all 10. There were no material disagreements requiring adjudication in this cohort. Descriptive primary means were clarity 5.00, completeness 4.83, and usefulness 3.93 on a 1–5 scale; secondary means were 5.00, 4.00, and 3.90 on its subset. On that same subset, primary means were 5.00, 4.70, and 3.90; completeness ratings differed despite agreement on material support. These subjective ratings are not calibrated performance measures.

The separate diagnostic repair sample contains **six pairs, twelve topics, two original runs, and one shared corpus**. Both reviewers assessed all twelve topics. Raw agreement was 12/12 on support, 12/12 on unsafe grouping, and 7/12 on the precise irrelevant-citation indices. A fresh third reviewer adjudicated the five citation disagreements from blinded evidence and rationales. A citation supporting an explicit prose clause remains relevant even when that clause is unsafely grouped under the headline; the final record keeps those problems separate.

The support regression remained after adjudication: a repair removed unsafe grouping while introducing an unsupported material claim. Thus cleaner grouping does not guarantee better grounding. These deliberately difficult diagnostic examples are separate from the reconstructed cohort and are not independent observations of deployed service reliability.

One reviewer also inspected both complete frozen corpora from September 30 and October 1 (49 and 194 items, with 18 and 22 selected topics). Both received selection-relevance ratings of 4/5. The reviewer identified two and four important omission clusters respectively, including federal-policy, content-access, election, and international-security developments. No selected topic was flagged as clearly misrouted or low-value. These are corpus-relative editorial judgments, not proof of complete news coverage, and this part was not double reviewed.
